01 What we've built

Lessons that move. Drawn live, voiced by Sunny.

Sunny teaches new ideas with short animated lessons, each one a little story on a live stage: cubes tip a balance, a cookie is cut into fair shares, a fraction wall lines up its pieces. Students make their own calls along the way, and Sunny talks them through every step.

  • Exact by construction

    Every picture is drawn from the lesson's exact numbers. Seven counters are always seven, and three fourths is always three of four equal parts.

  • Students make the call

    Lessons stop for "your turn" moments. A wrong pick gets a kind nudge and the lesson waits until the student finds the right one.

  • Help that climbs

    Stuck on a practice problem? Help steps up: a hint, then "watch how" with an example of the same kind, then "let's do yours together."

02 Inside Sunny

Reading they choose. Math at their pace.

Students pick what they love to read about, and Sunny writes stories and articles around it at exactly their level. Math practice adapts skill by skill. Parents see the whole picture; students see encouragement.

03 Why a harness

Fluency is not teaching.

AI can explain anything, and be wrong about it with exactly the same confidence. For an adult, that's an inconvenience. For a seven-year-old learning subtraction for the first time, a wrong answer delivered warmly is worse than no answer at all.

04 How Sunny works

Layers, not prompts.

Sunny treats the AI as one part of a larger system, not the whole thing. Here is one hint on its way to a second grader. Watch what happens when the AI's first draft gives the answer away.

  1. Curriculum

    Grade 2 · find the missing number

    49 + ? = 71
  2. Correctness

    Worked out by Sunny before the AI says a word

    ? = 22 49 → 50 → 71
  3. The AI writes

    Count up from 49 and you'll land on 22! Start at 49. How far is it to 50? Then how far from 50 to 71?

  4. Checks
    • Doesn't give the answer away
    • Every equation is true
    • Reads at a grade 2 level
    Sent back with the reason
  5. The student sees

    Start at 49. How far is it to 50? Then how far from 50 to 71?

If a rewrite still misses, Sunny falls back to a hint written by people. The AI never grades a math answer.

  1. 01

    Curriculum

    A map of 188 math skills, grade by grade, aligned to published state standards. Sunny chooses the next skill; the AI never decides what school looks like.

  2. 02

    Correctness

    The answer to every problem, and the steps to get there, are worked out before the AI is asked for a single word. Accuracy is settled before the lesson is written.

  3. 03

    Verification

    Everything the AI writes is checked for answer leaks, false arithmetic, invented claims and reading level before it's shown. Anything that falls short goes back with the reason.

  4. 04

    Grounding

    Lessons about the real world are built from vetted reference material rather than the AI's memory, so a student exploring a favorite subject gets something new, and true.

  5. 05

    Safety

    Anything a student types is screened before a lesson is written, with the bar set for their age. When in doubt, it fails closed. Safety comes before the writing, not after it.

  6. 06

    Mastery that sticks

    Mastery is measured by what a student keeps: a skill only counts as mastered after a hint-free review days later. Knowing something on Friday isn't the same as knowing it in March.

05 What's next In development

A picture for every "I don't get it."

Every student gets stuck somewhere different. We're teaching Sunny to draw on the spot: when a student is confused, Sunny works out what tripped them up, draws the picture that clears it up, and walks them through it step by step.

What Sunny noticed

06 Built to last

It gets better as AI does.

Sunny isn't built around any one AI. It can teach reading and math with different AI behind the scenes, held to the same high standards. When more capable AI arrives, Sunny evolves to teach students better without starting over.

The curriculum, the safety standards, the quality checks, and everything we've learned about how students actually learn are built to last.
